I research how compact AI models can produce outputs that are explainable and verifiable, applied in my PhD to personalised and trustworthy learning material, and I build AI systems deployed in the real world.

My PhD at Nottingham Trent University focuses on transitional learners, students moving between stages and/or educational environments. Methodologically, I work on compact LLMs (LoRA/QLoRA, GRPO reinforcement learning) that generate executable and renderable artefacts in interactive execution loops, so that what a model produces can be verified rather than taken on trust. My research across generative AI, explainable AI (XAI), and medical imaging has been published in venues including _IEEE Transactions on Multimedia_, the _NeurIPS 2026 Workshop on Foundations of LLM Post-Training_, _UKCI_, and _SN Computer Science_. Alongside the research, I build interactive explainability tools such as [Glass-box GPT](https://ravidusilva.com/glassbox-gpt/), which trains a tiny GPT in your browser and shows why it writes each character. I also lecture at NTU on mathematics, databases and C++.

Before the PhD, I led the R&D team at BooleenLab (formerly BooleanLabs) in Colombo, building commercial AI products for clients in the US, UK and Singapore. At Ensera Design (formerly Kinneir Dufort) in Bristol, I built the back end for AI-assisted breast cancer diagnosis, an AI classifier deployed across 80 Windows IoT kiosks, and embedded C/C++ drivers for STM32 microcontrollers.

I work mainly in Python, PyTorch, and C++, from mathematical formulation through to production deployment. Next, I want to take explainable, verifiable AI onto edge hardware and small robots. I will complete my PhD in late 2027 and am looking for research-focused roles in the UK from autumn 2027.

**Research interests**:

- Generative AI and compact LLMs for personalised education
- Execution-grounded reinforcement learning and LLM post-training
- Explainable and verifiable AI, especially for computer vision
- AI for medical imaging and digital health
- Edge AI: compact and quantized models on embedded and robotic hardware
